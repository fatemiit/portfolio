Product design · Fintech · Farabi

Fixing the watchlist traders kept fighting.

Adding a stock to a watchlist should take a second. In Farabi’s trading dashboard it took several steps and often ended in an error. The redesign cut task time by 42% and brought errors in stock management to zero.

My role as design lead

I led the product design team at Farabi that took on this problem. Hatam Alijanpour owned the design end to end, from the behavior analysis to validation testing.

01 · The problem

A simple task with a hidden rule.

Microsoft Clarity recordings and early usability tests told the same story: managing a watchlist cost far more effort than it should, and the system was rigid in ways users couldn’t see.

A list you couldn’t touch.

The default “Portfolio” list was automated and locked, but nothing said so. Traders kept trying to add or remove stocks there, and kept hitting errors.

Too many steps to add one stock.

Adding a stock meant picking a category first. Every extra decision slowed down a trader who only wanted to keep an eye on something.

No way to see what you were already watching.

To check whether a stock was already on a list, users had to switch between categories one by one. The system wasn’t showing its own status.

02 · The solution

Separate adding from browsing.

We restructured the information architecture so adding a stock no longer depended on which category you were looking at. You add from where you are, then choose where it goes.

Choose the lists, not the view.

When adding, the user picks from their own categories. The locked Portfolio list stays out of that choice, so there is nothing left to collide with.

One stock, many lists.

Multi-select lets a trader add a stock to several categories in a single action.

Show what’s already tracked.

Clear status indicators mark stocks that are already on a watchlist, so nobody has to go hunting through categories.

03 · Testing

Tested before, during and after.

Usability testing ran at every stage: before the redesign to find the friction, iteratively while refining the flow, and again after launch. In validation testing, both experienced traders and newcomers completed every scenario.

04 · The outcome

Faster, and without errors.

Average task completion time dropped by 42%. The error rate in stock management went to zero, and validation testing showed a 100% scenario completion rate across pro users and newcomers. The list-management pattern also became a base we could reuse for other complex financial data.

What I took from the project

Most errors are design decisions nobody explained.

The locked Portfolio list wasn’t a bug. It was a rule the interface never communicated. Making constraints visible, and designing them out of the path, did more than any error message could.
