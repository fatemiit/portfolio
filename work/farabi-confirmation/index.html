Product design · Fintech · Farabi

Designing confirmations for actions you can’t undo.

Users were deleting orders by mistake, and there was no undo. Reordering and re-weighting one confirmation sheet became a rule for every irreversible action in the Farabixo app.

My role Product design lead

Product Farabixo mobile trading app

Focus Error prevention

01 · The problem

A mistake with no way back.

Some time after Farabixo’s first release, we noticed a pattern: people were deleting orders they didn’t mean to delete. In a trading app that is not a small slip. A deleted order couldn’t be restored. The user had to build it again from scratch, and the market didn’t wait for them.

Undo was the obvious answer, but it wasn’t possible in the time we had. So the question became: if we can’t add a safety net after the mistake, how do we design the moment before it?

02 · Discovery

The risk was bigger than one button.

We started with session recordings and watched how people handled negative confirmations, such as deleting an order. The same risk showed up elsewhere: the sheet confirming logout, and the sheet for signing an electronic contract. Each one asked for a decision that was hard to reverse.

The pattern was consistent. The irreversible action was both the most prominent element and the first one in the reading path, so a fast or distracted tap landed on it.

The problem

Prevent errors in flows that are hard or impossible to reverse.

The success metric

Fewer users re-creating an item right after deleting it.

03 · The decision

Two rules for every irreversible action.

Instead of patching one sheet, we defined a rule that every irreversible confirmation in the app would follow.

Put the safe option first.

Cancel comes first in the user’s reading direction, so the first glance lands on the way out, not on the commitment.

Lower the weight of the commitment.

The irreversible action keeps its meaning, but it is no longer the heaviest thing on the sheet. The user has to look for it, which adds a moment of intent without adding a step.

04 · Applying it

Same rule, different intent.

The rule works for negative actions and for positive ones that are hard to take back.

Signing a digital contract

Agreeing to the terms is a positive action for the business, so it keeps the primary green button. Following the rule, it sits second, with the safe option first.

Deleting an order

Previously, the first option was a solid red delete button. Its weight and its position made accidental taps easy, and recovering meant re-entering the order. Now cancel comes first, and delete is lighter but still red, because deleting an order is not a neutral action.

What I took from the project

When you can’t add a safety net, design the moment before the mistake.

The change itself was small. What made it valuable was turning it into a rule the whole team could apply to every irreversible decision in the product, instead of a one-off fix to one sheet.
