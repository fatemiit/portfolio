Product strategy · WallGold

Designing gold to feel like money.

My role

When Wallex decided to enter the gold market, WallGold was a small new line. As Head of Product & Design, I set its product structure and architecture and formed the product, design, engineering and data teams. I led the shift to Gold as Currency and the redesign that followed; Hooman Ahmadi owned the product design. Five months after I started, I handed WallGold to its new Business Line Owner at three million users and moved on to own a new business line at Wallex.

How it started

Building a new line from the ground up.

Wallex decided to enter the gold market with WallGold. At the time it was a small line with a clear opportunity: make gold investment simple for people looking for ways to protect their savings.

I set the product structure and architecture, and formed the core teams across product, design, engineering and data. The goal for the first months was a product people could trust with their savings, and a team that could keep shipping as the line grew.

In its first months, WallGold grew with buying gold at the centre of the experience. The next question was what people could do with gold once they owned it.

01 · Strategy into design

Design for what happens after the purchase.

Gold as Currency asked a broader question than buying: once people own gold, what can they do with it? Transfer already existed. Gifting came up in early explorations, and a future bank card for spending gold was on the table.

Banking apps gave us a familiar language for that relationship: a balance, the actions attached to it, and a clear way to find services. Simplicity became the rule for deciding what people should see first.

Starting point

A place to buy gold, with the purchase as the defining moment.

Strategic direction

Gold as Currency: holding and moving gold as one account experience.

02 · Making the direction tangible

Bring an account-first draft into review.

The original home showed the gold balance and its toman value, then a large price chart that led into cash and installment buying. Its hierarchy was about acquiring gold.

The first draft grouped the balance with funding and reporting, and turned goals, installment progress, price and automatic saving into service cards. We took it to design review to test the strategy on real screens: could this feel like an account people would come back to?

03 · Refining the home

Make the balance the starting point.

The revised home put Buy gold and Deposit toman inside the balance card, so the account and its immediate actions read as one group. Price became a compact tile next to savings, installment purchase and credit.

The working rule was simple: help people understand their account, then help them choose an action.

04 · Room to grow

Give every feature a home, without crowding the home.

As WallGold added services, putting all of them on the home would make the account harder to read. We designed a feature hub: one place to discover services, so the home stayed focused and new capabilities had somewhere to land.

The hub also made the strategy visible. Transfer sat alongside physical delivery, saving, installment buying and toman credit.

05 · Choosing the first step

Start with what people could already do.

Early drafts explored gifting gold, including a birthday gift card. For the first step, we made the existing transfer feature more prominent in the hub. It connected the long-term strategy to something people could use right away.

We also designed with a possible bank card in mind, so the account and the hub could take that service later. Gifting and the card shaped how far ahead the structure needed to look; the step we shipped was transfer.

06 · Adapting to user needs

Let each tile reflect the state of its service.

The home used modular tiles. Illustrated tiles introduced services people hadn’t tried yet. Once a service was in use, its tile showed what mattered: the amount saved, a payment due date or installment progress.

The gold balance stayed the starting point in both states, so one structure supported both discovering services and managing them day to day.

07 · Carrying it into transactions

Focus on one amount at a time.

Buying and selling carried the same principle. The old entry screen had two boxed fields for toman and grams. The redesign gave one active amount the focus, kept its converted value nearby and added a dedicated keypad.

Purchase review moved from a bottom sheet to a full screen, with the summary at the top, bank cards listed below and the amount repeated on the final action.

08 · The outcome

Average gold transfer activity grew by 78%.

After the redesign, average transfer activity rose by 78% while purchase activity stayed flat. Transfer already existed, so the change came from people using a capability the product already had.

Online feedback also mentioned simplicity and satisfaction. These were qualitative signals rather than a formal score, and the design’s contribution wasn’t isolated.

It changed how we defined success. For a strategy about using gold, purchase growth alone would have missed the clearest change in behaviour.

Five months after I started, I handed WallGold to its new Business Line Owner at three million users and moved on to own a new business line at Wallex. Today it serves more than six million users with a team of over 100 people.

What I took from the project

A new strategy can change the meaning of an existing feature.

Gold as Currency gave us a reason to rethink hierarchy, navigation and transactions together. Transfer made it tangible: the feature already existed, and giving it a clear role was the first step toward the bigger ambition.
