Turning dead space into a trading command center.

The most visible part of our trading app’s home screen said hello and nothing else. We turned it into a widget system that put market movement one gesture away.

My role as design lead

I set the vision for this direction and framed the problem: the header had to become a scalable answer, not a one-off widget. I shaped the information architecture behind the two gestures, and worked with the business to grow the pattern over time and keep it in sync with Farabi’s other business lines. Hatam owned the product design end to end.

01 · The problem

Prime space, zero value.

The header of the Farabi mobile dashboard showed a greeting. In a trading app, that is the most valuable real estate on the screen, and it wasn’t helping anyone decide anything.

Looking at it as a team, three problems stood out.

Market context was buried.

Gainers and losers lived deeper in the app. To read the market, traders had to leave the home screen first, which slowed down the moment that mattered most: the decision to trade.

The business had nowhere calm to speak.

New features needed promoting, and the usual tools were pop-ups and banners that interrupt. There was no quiet, consistent place on the screen for them.

Nothing we could scale.

Whatever we built had to work beyond one screen and stay consistent across the product.

The goal

Make the header earn its space: faster trading decisions and higher engagement.

How we’d measure it

Header engagement, session duration, and time from opening the app to a trade.

02 · Exploration

Two gestures, two depths of information.

We benchmarked how other trading and finance apps use the top of their home screens, then worked through options in whiteboard sessions. The hard part was information architecture: showing dense market data without turning the home screen into a terminal.

Low-fidelity wireframes tested two interactions.

Swipe for the summary.

A horizontal carousel of summary cards and promotional banners. Quick to scan, and a quiet place for the business to introduce new features.

Pull down for the detail.

A vertical expand that reveals detailed analytics only when someone asks for them, so the default screen stays clean.

Testing both early gave us one pattern that worked on Home and Portfolio, not a one-off for a single page.

03 · The design

A command center you can read at a glance.

In high fidelity, the job was hierarchy: making complex financial data readable in a second or two.

The key decision was putting the “Gainers and Losers” chart inside the expanding header. Market movement now lives on the home screen, one pull away, instead of several taps deep.

We kept the chart a simple horizontal bar chart, green for gains and red for losses. In trading, familiar conventions matter. Nobody should have to learn a legend to read the market.

04 · The outcome

From a greeting to the busiest part of the screen.

After launch, the header became something people actually used. Daily engagement with it rose by 12%, average session duration grew by 7%, and the time from opening the app to placing a trade dropped by 15%.

The swipe area also gave the business a visible but non-intrusive place to promote new features, and the pattern became part of a shared design system across the mobile and web apps.

What I took from the project

The best place to add value is often space you already have.

We didn’t add a feature or a new screen. We took the most visible area of the app, which was doing nothing, and made it work for both the trader and the business.
